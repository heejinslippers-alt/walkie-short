# "What If Everyone Jumped at the Same Time?" — script v1 (2026-11-20)
~65 s vertical short, Kokoro am_puck at 1.15. Mom (the channel mascot) leads the countdown and ends it stuck in traffic.

## Facts (with sources)
- World population about 8.2-8.3 billion in 2026 (PRB 2026 data sheet 8.24 billion; US Census Bureau 8.2 billion).
  https://www.prb.org/app/uploads/2026/10/10.20.26_PRB_World-Population-Data-Sheet_Key-Findings-Brief.pdf
- Total human biomass about 390-420 million tonnes (Weizmann Institute / PNAS; Our World in Data check 7.95 bn x 50 kg = 398 Mt).
  https://ourworldindata.org/wild-mammals-birds-biomass
- Earth's mass 5.97 x 10^24 kg, so ~15 trillion times humanity's ~4 x 10^11 kg. A 0.5 m jump moves the Earth by about
  0.5 x (4e11 / 6e24) = 3 x 10^-14 m: well under a thousandth of an atom's width (~10^-10 m). It moves back when everyone lands.
- 8.2 billion people at about 5 per square metre need ~1,650 km2; Greater London is ~1,570 km2 ("not much bigger than London").
- Beast Quake, 8 Jan 2011: Seahawks fans celebrating Marshawn Lynch's touchdown registered about magnitude 2 on a seismometer near the stadium.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beast_Quake
- 8.2 billion / ~68,000 (stadium crowd) is about 120,000 crowds.

## Script
What if every person on Earth jumped at exactly the same time? Could we shove the planet off course?
There are eight point two billion of us, weighing about four hundred million tonnes all together.
So we all squeeze into one place. Packed shoulder to shoulder, everybody fits in a space not much bigger than London.
Three. Two. One. Jump!
Eight billion people leave the ground, and the whole Earth moves...
by less than a thousandth of the width of an atom.
Because the planet is fifteen trillion times heavier than all of us put together.
It's like a flea jumping off a cruise ship. The ship doesn't even notice.
Luckily, when we land, the Earth moves right back.
Unluckily, the landing is loud. In twenty eleven, Seattle football fans celebrated a touchdown so hard, a seismometer recorded it as a small earthquake.
Now picture a hundred and twenty thousand of those crowds landing at once.
But the real disaster comes afterwards.
Eight billion people, in one place, all trying to get home at the same time.
And we already know how that ends.
