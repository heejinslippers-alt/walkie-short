# "The Building That Cooked a Car" — original script v1 (2026-11-17)
Format: ~60 s vertical short, ~3.7 words/s, one steady narrator, our own characters and story.

## Facts (with sources)
- 20 Fenchurch Street, London ("the Walkie-Talkie"), designed by Rafael Viñoly, still under construction in late summer 2013.
  Its south face is concave, so it focused reflected sunlight onto Eastcheap below.
- A parked Jaguar XJ (owner Martin Lindsay) had its wing mirror, badge and panels melted in about an hour.
- A newspaper reporter fried an egg in a pan on the pavement using only the reflected heat.
- A barber shop reported its doormat/carpet scorched. Press nicknames: "Walkie-Scorchie", "Fryscraper".
- Reported temperatures in the beam ranged from ~70 °C to ~91 °C (accounts vary). Nobody was hurt.
- Fix: a temporary screen, then a permanent sunshade of horizontal fins over the glass (2014).
- Viñoly's Vdara hotel in Las Vegas (opened 2008) had the same problem: in 2010 guests by the pool complained of singed hair and melted plastic bags ("the Vdara death ray").

## Script (~205 words, ~56 s)
A London skyscraper melted a parked car without ever touching it.
It's 2013, and the City's shiny new tower isn't even finished yet.
Down below, a man parks his Jaguar on the street and pops out for an hour.
When he comes back, the wing mirror and the badge have melted.
The next day, a reporter puts a frying pan on the pavement, cracks an egg into it, and it fries.
Across the road, a barber's doormat starts to smoke.
Then somebody finally looks up.
The whole glass front of the tower is pointing one blazing spot of sunlight straight at the street.
Luckily, nobody got hurt.
Unluckily, the egg did.
Ever used a magnifying glass to focus the sun into one tiny burning dot?
This tower's face curves inwards like a giant mirror, so every window bounces its sunlight to the same patch of road.
So the owners bolted a huge sunshade of metal fins across the front, and London's newest landmark had to wear sunglasses.
And the architect? He'd seen this before.
His hotel in Las Vegas had already been melting tourists' shopping bags by the pool.
